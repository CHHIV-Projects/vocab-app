# 1. Objective

M004.6.7 follows the completed M004.6.6 expanded screening validation.

M004.6.6 established that the simple screening architecture:

`nomic-embed-text + definition-focused-v1 + global cosine threshold 0.90`

does NOT meet the required high-recall generalization standard.

On the expanded Product Owner / Architect-adjudicated reference:

- resolved MERGE pairs: 30;
- resolved KEEP SEPARATE pairs: 164;
- UNCERTAIN pairs: 6;
- threshold 0.90 retained only 27/30 approved merges;
- merge recall: 90.0%;
- keep-separate reduction: 53.0%;
- one approved merge scored as low as 0.8273;
- lowering the threshold to 0.86 still lost one approved merge while reducing only 26.8% of resolved keep-separate comparisons;
- Top-K 3 recovered 100% merge recall but reduced keep-separate comparisons by only 16.5%;
- threshold 0.90 UNION Top-K 3 also reached 100% recall but reduced only 15.9%;
- therefore the existing embedding screen does not save enough work at the required safety level to justify production use.

This milestone must NOT attempt to rescue the failed screening design automatically.

The objective of M004.6.7 is:

> Characterize why approved learner-meaning merges are being missed, then compare a small number of fundamentally different candidate-reduction strategies to determine whether exhaustive single-pair Qwen can be made practical without sacrificing semantic safety.

The central architectural principle remains:

> AI decides bounded semantics. Python owns identity, eligibility, topology, completeness, persistence, and final acceptance.

# 15. Alternative Candidate Reduction — Bounded Partition Candidate Proposal

This converts the learned task from:

“which pairs deserve expensive semantic comparison?”

# 22. Union Safety Interpretation

A false-positive candidate proposal costs only an extra Qwen pair judgment.

A false-negative candidate proposal can create a false split by preventing semantic review.

Therefore candidate proposal should optimize high recall.

If using multiple proposal attempts diagnostically, evaluate:

`union of proposed candidate pairs`

because union increases recall at the cost of more pairwise work.

Do not confuse candidate proposal precision with final merge precision.

# 29. Lazy Consolidation Versus Full Precomputation

Analyze two operational models.

## A. Lazy per-word consolidation

User searches an unsynthesized word:

- retrieve lexical evidence;
- consolidate that word;
- cache validated result.

Advantages:

- no full lexical precomputation;
- compute only words actually requested.

Cost:

- first lookup may take seconds/minutes on large partitions.

## B. Background/precomputed consolidation

Process lexical entries ahead of demand.

Advantages:

- immediate learner lookup after build.

Cost:

- potentially enormous whole-corpus inference cost.

Do not implement either.

Estimate which is more realistic given measured pair counts.

A hybrid may also be reasonable:

- lazy foreground;
- optional background warming later.
