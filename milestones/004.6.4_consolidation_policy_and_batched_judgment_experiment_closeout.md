## 3. Reference policy and evidence

The explicit benchmark policy is:

> Merge only near-paraphrase, duplicate, or contextual-application variants. Keep specialized, broader/narrower, related-but-distinct, materially different, and uncertain senses separate. Prefer false splits.

The reference contains 58 source-provenance pairs from four complete POS/material partitions: `charge` noun (25), `running` adjective (15), `cleave` verb (15), and `quark` noun (3). It contains six policy-reference merges and 52 keep-separate decisions across duplicate, contextual, specialized, broader/narrower, related, and materially distinct examples.

Reference status is deliberately:

Accordingly, every accuracy, precision, recall, false-merge, and false-split figure below means **agreement with this provisional product-policy reference**, not linguistic ground truth or completed human semantic review. Source sense IDs, definitions, labels, examples, relations, expected decisions, and reference reasons are retained in `reference_pairs.json`.

## 4. Refined bounded contract

The model can answer only:

```json
{
  "decision": "merge | keep_separate | uncertain",
  "reason": "paraphrase_or_duplicate | contextual_variant | specialized_subsense | broader_or_narrower | related_but_distinct | materially_different | insufficient_evidence"
}
```

For batched calls, Python provides fixed pair IDs and the schema requires every supplied key with `additionalProperties: false`. Code accepts only exact pair coverage and schema-valid closed-enum values. It treats `decision == "merge"` as the only positive merge evidence; a reason never creates a merge.

`merge` paired with `specialized_subsense`, `broader_or_narrower`, `related_but_distinct`, or `materially_different` is retained as a policy-consistency flag, not hidden as a transport/schema failure. There were no structural failures in the final run, while such diagnostic flags remained visible.

Every raw attempt records benchmark ID/version, run/timestamp/HEAD, model/digest/Ollama version, request-local invocation options, pair context/source IDs, hashes, raw provider response, parsed response, diagnostics, and timing.

Python generated 31 deterministic anchor proposal records per model in `anchor_proposals.json`. It never accepts those as global containers. It retains direct merge suggestions, then applies complete-link checks before marking a tentative member safe: GPT-OSS has 5 missing-evidence and 6 explicit-separation blocks; Qwen has 2 explicit-separation blocks. Each model had seven direct-merge proposals and seven multi-sense safety-accepted tentative proposals, but they can overlap and are explicitly `accepted_as_global_container: false`.

Anchor quality drops materially from the single-pair baseline and produces more provisional false merges. It reduces calls but introduces unacceptable semantic risk pending human review.

## 12. Direct architectural answers

1. **Refined contract better calibrated?** It is diagnostically better and lets Qwen express merges; a matched semantic-control experiment was not run, so universal superiority over M004.6.3 is unproven.
2. **Does GPT-OSS remain more useful?** Not on this provisional reference: Qwen has better single-pair and every measured batch agreement, fewer single-pair false merges, and lower latency.
3. **Does Qwen improve?** Provisionally yes: eight refined single-pair merges, 93.1% agreement, and 83.3% merge recall replace M004.6.3's all-distinct sample.
4. **Does batching preserve quality?** No measured batch preserves the single-pair baseline.
5. **Best quality/cost batch?** Qwen batch-10 is the best measured batched compromise, but its quality loss and false merges prevent approval.
6. **Does anchor reduce requests materially?** Yes, 46.6% versus single-pair.
7. **Does anchor introduce unacceptable risk?** Provisionally yes: 72.4% agreement and 12--14 false merges.
8. **Is complete-link safer?** Yes by deterministic policy: it preserves all invariants and blocks missing/contradictory cross evidence. The measured incremental effect is one additional block.
9. **Large-partition projections?** Reported in Section 11.
10. **Are batching/anchor sufficient to avoid embeddings?** No conclusion: they reduce calls but have not shown acceptable semantic quality for safe scale-up.
11. **What gap would embeddings solve?** A future screener must reduce clearly non-merge comparisons on large partitions while retaining essentially all Product-Owner-validated merge candidates; this experiment has not yet measured such recall, so no threshold is authorized.
12. **Is this ready for production planning?** No. The reference requires Product Owner / Architect semantic validation, and no strategy has both the required quality and demonstrated large-partition operating model.

## 13. Embedding decision and next step

**Embeddings are not currently justified.** The evidence shows a scalability pressure but does not satisfy the required quality gate for introducing a screener: batching and anchor paths are not semantically accepted, and the reference labels remain provisional. Fine-tuning, multi-model routing, runtime migration, and production integration remain unauthorized.

The immediate next action is Product Owner / Architect review of `review.csv`, `clusters.json`, and `anchor_proposals.json`. Only after validating the policy reference and choosing acceptable false-merge tolerances should the project authorize either an architecture-selection plan or a tightly measured embedding-screening experiment with a specific merge-recall/comparison-reduction target.
