## 5. Fixture provenance and categories

All fixtures are **benchmark-reconstructed**, not retained production grouping-stage artifacts. The harness performs read-only exact lexical lookup; for each materially populated POS (at least two unique glosses), it deterministically takes at most six unique source glosses, assigns local code-owned `G001...` IDs, and hashes the fixture.

Each fixture records source word, normalized lemma, POS, Wiktionary dataset version, lexical evidence hash, construction policy/method, candidate IDs/definitions, fixture hash, and categories. Candidate groups are exactly the same for both models and both contracts.

This construction is suitable for core-selection comparison, but its source-gloss candidates are not semantically consolidated production groups. This is the principal fixture limitation and is why human semantic review is required.

## 8. Stability results

Stability is calculated per `fixture × model × contract` using final **sets** (not order), distinct set count, exact five-attempt agreement, and mean pairwise Jaccard similarity.

| Model | Contract | All-five agreement | Unstable fixtures | Mean per-fixture Jaccard |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GPT-OSS | subset array | 30 / 36 | 6 | 0.981 |
| GPT-OSS | fixed key | 21 / 36 | 15 | 0.922 |
| Qwen, no-thinking | subset array | 36 / 36 | 0 | 1.000 |
| Qwen, no-thinking | fixed key | 32 / 36 | 4 | 0.980 |

The observed GPT-OSS fixed-key instability clusters in reconstructed high-polysemy/boundary cases such as `bank`, `charge`, `run`, `set`, and `mouse`; GPT-OSS subset-array instability was narrower (`awful`, `cleave`, `quark`, `running`, `set`). Qwen fixed-key variation appeared for `bank`, `running`, and `set`; Qwen subset-array selections were set-stable for this data.

These are small, reconstructed-fixture exploratory observations—not a semantic-quality judgment. The anonymous review package exposes the underlying candidates and selections for human assessment of whether the variations are legitimate boundary choices or meaning loss.

## 9. Performance results

Ollama response fields were summarized by model and contract. p95 is a simple artifact-set percentile; load values are naturally observed, not dedicated profiling.

| Model / contract | p50 total | p95 total | Min–max | p50 prompt eval | p50 decode eval | p50 generated tokens | Max load |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GPT-OSS subset | 0.908 s | 5.276 s | 0.601–5.667 s | 0.007 s | 0.150 s | 20 | 4.685 s |

| Qwen subset | 0.408 s | 3.296 s | 0.258–3.328 s | 0.007 s | 0.271 s | 36.5 | 2.906 s |
| Qwen fixed key | 0.469 s | 0.687 s | 0.288–0.704 s | 0.007 s | 0.336 s | 47.5 | 0.142 s |

Qwen remained materially faster in total p50 latency for this bounded task, although its decode-only duration and emitted-token counts were higher. Fixed-key added a modest cost:

- GPT-OSS: +0.214 s p50 and +17 p50 tokens;
- Qwen: +0.061 s p50 and +11 p50 tokens.

The larger subset-array p95 values coincide with 36 load durations above one second for each model/contract's initial-of-fixture subset calls, indicating natural model lifecycle/load activity as the runner alternated model groups. The fixed-key companion calls were warm. No special model swapping experiment was performed.
