## 4. Wiktionary source options reviewed

### Raw Wiktionary XML plus local extraction

- Source: monthly `enwiktionary-<date>-pages-articles.xml.bz2` dump.
- Extraction: run Wiktextract locally with selected English and translingual capture.
- Complexity: highest; requires parser/runtime maintenance, temporary extraction storage, and substantial CPU/RAM.
- Update: monthly dump replacement and re-extraction.
- Field completeness: strongest control because extractor options are owned locally.
- Provenance: strong when dump date, Wiktextract commit, Wikitextprocessor commit, configuration, and importer schema are recorded.
- Lookup: poor without a second indexing step.
- License: source Wiktionary content is generally CC BY-SA 4.0/GFDL subject to source-specific notices and attribution obligations.
- Operational simplicity: low to medium.

### Wiktextract-derived structured data

- Source: Wiktextract JSONL generated from the English dump with `capture_language_codes=["en", "mul"]`.
- Complexity: medium; extraction is delegated to the maintained Wiktextract/Wikitextprocessor tool.
- Update: rerun the extraction for a new monthly dump and atomically replace the versioned artifact/index.
- Field completeness: high for glosses, senses, POS, forms, pronunciation, examples, etymology, and linkages where source markup supplies them.
- Provenance: strong if the dump date, extractor commits, configuration, and per-record source identity are stored.
- Lookup: good after building a local index; JSONL itself is not the runtime lookup structure.
- License: inherits Wiktionary reuse/attribution obligations; the Wiktextract code is MIT, but code and extracted content licensing are separate concerns.
- Operational simplicity: medium and controllable.

### Kaikki/Wiktextract pre-extracted English data

- Source: Kaikki pre-extracted JSONL.
- Complexity: lowest for initial adoption.
- Update: download replacement snapshots from Kaikki; version and verify the snapshot.
- Field completeness: high and demonstrated by the published structured entry examples.
- Provenance: good when snapshot metadata is retained, but dependent on the distributor's post-processing and availability.
- Lookup: good after indexing; published English machine-readable data is approximately 3.0GB and currently marked deprecated on the English page.
- License: requires Wiktionary attribution/share-alike compliance plus clear attribution for the extraction/distribution source.
- Operational simplicity: high initially, but the deprecation notice creates a lifecycle risk.

## 5. Preferred Wiktionary-derived source

Recommendation: use **Wiktextract-generated, English-focused JSONL from the monthly English Wiktionary dump**, with `en` and `mul` capture and a recorded extractor configuration.

Reason:

- avoids dependence on the currently deprecated Kaikki postprocessed English download;
- avoids writing and maintaining a custom raw XML parser;
- preserves rich structured fields and source provenance;
- permits English-only extraction while retaining language/source-language/borrowing metadata;
- supports reproducible monthly replacement;
- keeps the raw evidence artifact versioned independently from the application image.

Kaikki/Wiktextract pre-extracted data remains a useful fallback for reconnaissance or an emergency source, not the preferred long-term operational contract.

## 6. Dataset, storage, and update estimates

Observed public order-of-magnitude evidence:

- Kaikki English postprocessed JSONL page: approximately 3.0GB; the page marks this file deprecated.
- Kaikki raw Wiktextract all-language data from the English edition: approximately 2.7GB compressed and 23.1GB uncompressed.
- Wiktionary audio bulk archive: approximately 20.4GB; M004.2 does not recommend importing it because gTTS remains the audible provider.
- Wiktextract documentation states extraction of all English-edition data can take about 1.25 hours on a 128-core EPYC system and scales with substantial memory per worker; a household server should expect materially longer extraction.

For the recommended English-focused extraction, exact filtered sizes were not benchmarked because M004.2 explicitly forbids importing a production dataset. Planning estimates should reserve:

- compressed source/download: low single-digit GB order of magnitude;
- extracted versioned JSONL: several GB order of magnitude until a real filtered sample is measured;
- SQLite/FTS index: hundreds of MB to a few GB depending on retained examples, etymology, forms, and provenance payloads;
- temporary build storage: at least source plus extracted plus index, with additional working headroom;
- Docker image impact: near zero if data is kept in a host-owned lexical volume;
- runtime RAM: bounded lookup should be tens to a few hundred MB for an indexed SQLite process, not the entire JSONL in memory;
- import/index/update: hours rather than seconds for a full refresh on household hardware; lookup itself should be milliseconds to low hundreds of milliseconds once indexed.

These are planning ranges, not benchmark claims. M004.3 must measure the selected configuration using a small sample before committing to full production import.

The lexical dataset must not be copied into the normal application image. Store immutable versioned artifacts and the active index in a dedicated host-owned lexical-data volume or separate data service, with an atomic active-version switch.

## 7. Licensing and attribution

Wiktionary text is generally available under CC BY-SA 4.0 and GFDL terms as described by Wikimedia reuse policy, with source-specific notices potentially applying. CC BY-SA requires appropriate credit, a license link, indication of changes, and share-alike treatment for adapted material. Wiktionary/Wikimedia attribution should be accompanied by the relevant source snapshot date and links.

Wiktextract code is MIT-licensed, but that does not replace the licensing obligations of the extracted Wiktionary content. The extracted dataset/distribution source must be credited separately where required.

Recommended Vocab treatment:

- maintain a versioned attribution/license document beside lexical dataset metadata;
- record Wiktionary, Wikimedia, Wiktextract, and dataset snapshot links;
- state that data was extracted/normalized and identify the snapshot date;
- provide attribution in an About/help surface and a durable operator/developer documentation file;
- preserve per-entry source links where practical for later UI provenance;
- have the Product Owner review final attribution wording before production import.

This is an architecture conclusion, not legal advice.

## 8. Fields reliably available

### Reliably structured

- word/lemma;
- language and language code;
- part of speech;
- sense list and glosses;
- source entry grouping;
- many forms where Wiktionary supplies inflection data;
- source dataset/version metadata.

### Available but variable

- examples and quotations;
- usage labels and topics;
- pronunciation IPA and dialect tags;
- audio references;
- etymology text/templates;
- synonyms, antonyms, hypernyms, related and derived terms;
- borrowing/source-language metadata;
- redirects and alternative forms.

### Highly irregular or optional

- complete inflection paradigms;
- consistent sense identifiers across all entries;
- complete etymologies;
- systematic holonym/meronym data;
- reliable coverage for every learner-relevant synonym or usage label;
- uniform examples and regional metadata.

Missing source evidence must remain missing. The future system must not fill absent fields from model memory.

## 10. Evidence identity and provenance

Recommend immutable evidence IDs with explicit components:

```text
WIK:<dataset-version>:<importer-version>:<language>:<entry-key>:<sense-key>:<field>
WN:<wordnet-version>:<synset-id>:<relation>
```

The exact encoding may use a canonical hash of the structured identity, but the source components must remain queryable. Do not use line numbers or import order.

Every lexical artifact should also record:

- source URL or dump identifier;
- dump/snapshot date;
- extractor and importer versions;
- normalization schema version;
- record hash;
- source locator/page title where available.

If an upstream entry changes or disappears, its old evidence version remains immutable and addressable. Accepted entries retain their evidence-set identity and do not silently point to the newest import.

## 10.1 Accepted evidence bundle retention

Accepted entries do not require indefinite retention of every complete historical multi-GB Wiktionary JSONL or SQLite dataset.

For each accepted derived vocabulary entry, preserve an immutable canonical evidence bundle containing the exact normalized source evidence used to create that entry:

```text
AcceptedEntry
  ->
DerivedSynthesis
  ->
EvidenceBundle
```

The bundle should retain, as applicable:

- Wiktionary lexical-entry evidence used;
- Wiktionary sense/gloss evidence used;
- forms used;
- etymology evidence used;
- pronunciation/IPA evidence used;
- relevant WordNet synsets/relations used;
- evidence IDs;
- source dataset versions;
- importer/schema versions;
- source locators;
- source-record hashes.

The evidence-set hash must be computed from a deterministic canonical serialization of this frozen bundle.

Lexical reference datasets remain large, replaceable, versioned reference data. The deployment may retain the active dataset and optionally rollback versions, with older complete versions eventually pruned under a defined retention policy.

Accepted evidence bundles are small, immutable application provenance retained with accepted application state and sufficient to reconstruct exactly which lexical evidence the synthesis and validator used. PostgreSQL is the preferred location for accepted-entry state and its immutable evidence/provenance bundle unless M004.3 discovers a concrete implementation reason to use an equivalent durable application-state representation.

No PostgreSQL schema change is authorized in M004.2. M004.3 must design and validate the concrete representation before full-scale lexical import.

## 11. WordNet's supplemental role

Treat Wiktionary and WordNet as parallel evidence:

- Wiktionary: learner-facing dictionary senses, forms, examples, etymology, pronunciation metadata, and source linkages;
- WordNet: synsets, semantic relations, and supplemental synonym/concept structure.

Do not assume a Wiktionary sense equals a WordNet synset. Any later cross-source relationship must be explicit and labeled as evidence-backed, heuristic, or unresolved. M004.3 should not require automatic sense alignment unless a bounded evaluation proves it reliable.

## 12. gTTS pronunciation role

Wiktionary-derived IPA and dialect metadata are written pronunciation evidence. gTTS remains the actual audible pronunciation service and the consistent voice used by Vocab.

A future UI may display IPA as source evidence while using gTTS for playback. The two must not be presented as though they are the same pronunciation artifact.

## 13. GPT-OSS derived-data contract

A later GPT-OSS synthesis milestone should accept a frozen evidence set and return structured derived data such as:

```json
{
  "word": "example",
  "parts_of_speech": [
    {
      "pos": "noun",
      "senses": [
        {
          "definition": "supported learner-facing wording",
          "source_evidence_ids": ["WIK:..."],
          "validation_status": "validated"
        }
      ]
    }
  ],
  "synonym_groups": [
    {
      "label": "supported sense label",
      "terms": ["..."],
      "source_evidence_ids": ["WIK:...", "WN:..."]
    }
  ],
  "forms": [],
  "etymology_summary": null,
  "usage_notes": [],
  "metadata": {
    "evidence_set_hash": "...",
    "source_versions": [],
    "model": "gpt-oss:20b",
    "prompt_version": "...",
    "validation_status": "validated"
  }
}
```

Required rules:

- every factual item carries supporting evidence IDs;
- source versions, model, prompt version, and evidence-set hash are recorded;
- unsupported factual output is rejected or omitted;
- presentation wording may be generated only from supported facts;
- model confidence is never treated as evidence.

## 14. Deterministic validation boundary

Ordinary code can enforce:

- valid JSON/schema;
- bounded output size;
- known POS values from the evidence set;
- every evidence ID exists in the frozen evidence set;
- every claim has at least one supporting evidence ID;
- no unknown source/version IDs;
- synonym terms occur in the cited Wiktionary/WordNet evidence;
- etymology and usage claims map to cited fields;
- no malformed source mapping;
- model/prompt/evidence versions are recorded;
- accepted output is tied to an immutable evidence-set hash.

Ordinary code cannot prove:

- that a paraphrase preserves every nuance;
- that selected senses are pedagogically optimal;
- that an etymology summary is elegant or complete;
- that a synonym group is ideal for a learner.

Those are quality-review concerns, not evidence-authority checks. An AI critic may later advise, but cannot certify itself.

## 15. Saved-entry stability

Recommend three versioned layers:

```text
source evidence version
        -> derived synthesis version
        -> accepted entry version
```

PostgreSQL should store user/application state and accepted-entry metadata, including:

- accepted entry ID;
- evidence-set hash/version;
- synthesis version;
- model and prompt version;
- acceptance timestamp;
- accepted payload or immutable reference to it.

Updating Wiktionary, WordNet, GPT-OSS, or prompts must not alter an accepted entry automatically. Refresh is explicit and creates a new candidate/derived version for review.

## 16. Physical storage/index recommendation

Recommended split:

- PostgreSQL: saved vocabulary, scores/history, accepted entry versions, synthesis/provenance metadata;
- versioned lexical evidence artifacts: compressed/source JSONL outside Git and outside the app image;
- SQLite/FTS5 active index: local lexical lookup over the selected English evidence, stored in a dedicated persistent lexical-data volume.

Why SQLite/FTS5:

- simple local deployment;
- replaceable as a complete versioned artifact;
- no additional network service;
- supports exact lemma lookup and future text search;
- keeps large reference data separate from operational PostgreSQL;
- easy atomic replacement by switching an active-version path.

A structured JSONL artifact remains the recoverable evidence source; SQLite is the lookup acceleration layer, not the sole authority.

## 18. Risks and unresolved decisions

- Full extraction is materially larger than the current application and needs a separate host storage/import workflow.
- The English Kaikki postprocessed download is marked deprecated; the recommended direct Wiktextract approach has higher operational complexity.
- Exact filtered English JSONL/index sizes and import timings require a representative fixture benchmark in M004.3.
- Wiktionary content quality and field coverage vary; missing data is expected.
- Licensing/attribution wording requires Product Owner/legal review before production distribution.
- WordNet/Wiktionary sense alignment remains unresolved by design.
- PostgreSQL schema changes for accepted-entry versioning are intentionally deferred.
