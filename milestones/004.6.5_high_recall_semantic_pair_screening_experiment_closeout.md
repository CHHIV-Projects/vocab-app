## 3. Screening architecture

The benchmark flow was:

```text
canonical source senses
  -> deterministic POS/material partitioning
  -> definition-focused embedding similarity
  -> threshold or symmetric top-K candidate retention
  -> retained Qwen single-pair semantic review
  -> code-owned conservative topology (future downstream boundary)
```

The screener never emits `merge`, container IDs, or topology. Its decisions are represented as:

- `retained_for_semantic_review`; or
- `screened_out_not_semantically_compared`.

Screened-out pairs are not treated as model-confirmed `keep_separate`. They simply have no positive merge evidence and therefore remain separate under the deterministic architecture.

All comparisons stayed within deterministic POS/material partitions. No cross-POS or prohibited material-label comparison was introduced.

## 7. Threshold sweep

The deterministic threshold sweep used `0.20` through `0.90`. The strongest threshold satisfying 100% resolved-merge recall was **0.90**:

| Threshold | Resolved merge recall | Keep-separate reduction | Lost resolved merges |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 0.75 | 100% | 3.8% | none |
| 0.80 | 100% | 26.9% | none |
| 0.85 | 100% | 55.8% | none |
| **0.90** | **100%** | **86.5%** | **none** |

At threshold 0.90, 45 of 52 resolved keep-separate reference pairs were screened out, four resolved merge pairs were retained, and both uncertain pairs were retained or excluded only according to their similarity without entering hard metrics. The threshold retained 12 of the 58 reference rows overall.

Threshold behavior is steep and reference-dependent. A threshold above 0.90 was not selected or extrapolated. Any threshold change requires a new versioned policy evaluation.

## 8. Top-K screening

Top-K was symmetric and deterministic:

- self-pairs excluded;
- candidates sorted by descending cosine, then source-sense ID;
- a pair retained if either endpoint selected the other;
- all available peers retained when fewer than K exist.

| K | Resolved merge recall | Keep-separate reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 100% | 21.2% |
| 3 | 100% | 5.8% |
| 5 | 100% | 0.0% |

Threshold 0.90 dominates the tested top-K policies on this reference, but top-K remains a useful deterministic alternative when global score calibration is not stable.

No hybrid threshold + top-K policy was added because the primary representation already met the reference recall/reduction gate and the prompt requires a narrow experiment.

## 9. Leave-one-word-out generalization

Threshold selection used only non-held-out words. The selected training threshold was 0.90 in each fold:

| Held-out word | Resolved merge pairs | Held-out merge recall | Training keep-separate reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `charge` | 1 | 100% | 79.3% |
| `cleave` | 2 | 100% | 97.4% |
| `running` | 1 | 100% | 82.1% |
| `quark` | 0 | not applicable | 85.7% |

This is encouraging but weak evidence: only three words have resolved merges, and `quark` contributes no positive merge example. It does not justify treating 0.90 as a lexicon-wide production threshold.

## 12. Storage and rebuild analysis

For approximately 1.9M production senses and 768 dimensions:

- float32 vectors: approximately 5.85 GB;
- float16 vectors: approximately 2.92 GB;
- plus metadata, IDs, hashes, partition indexes, and format overhead.

The measured 217-sense batch took 8.91 seconds including the local embedding request. A naive linear projection is approximately 24.4 senses/second, or approximately 21.6 hours for 1.9M senses. This projection assumes similar batching, uninterrupted service, and no additional I/O or model-loading costs; it is not a production SLA. GPU use was whatever policy the existing Ollama host selected; no GPU/runtime setting was changed. Rebuilding is required whenever the embedding model, digest, representation template, normalization, dimension, or precision changes.

The artifact/versioning cost is non-trivial but modest compared with exhaustive semantic calls if the high-partition reductions generalize. That conclusion remains conditional on expanded reference validation.

## 13. Answers to the architectural questions

1. **100% resolved merge retention?** Yes, for the four resolved merge pairs in the adjudicated reference at threshold 0.90.
2. **Maximum keep-separate reduction at that recall?** 86.5% on the resolved reference pairs.
3. **Global threshold?** 0.90 for this versioned representation/reference.
4. **Threshold sensitivity?** Reduction rises from 3.8% at 0.75 to 86.5% at 0.90 while retaining all four merges; the reference is too small to establish stability above 0.90.
5. **Does top-K outperform?** No on this reference; K=2 retained all merges but reduced only 21.2%.
6. **Does hybrid improve safety/generalization?** Not tested; deliberately omitted because the primary representation met the gate and strategy proliferation was out of scope.
7. **Hardest merge?** `P030` at 0.9045.
8. **Highest-similarity keep-separate cases?** `P052`, `P054`, `P049`, and `P050`; these are closely related cleave senses and show why screening cannot merge.
9. **Technical/specialized problem?** Yes. Cleave technical/general boundaries are high-similarity false-retention cases.
10. **Grammatical distinctions?** The small reference includes grammatical/contextual distinctions, but evidence is insufficient for a lexicon-wide conclusion.
11. **Large-partition reduction?** 81.5–97.6% in the requested projections.
12. **Projected Qwen calls?** For the reference, 36 instead of 174. For large partitions, applying the measured ratios would retain approximately 10 bank, 123 runner, 18 set, and 259 run pairs before the three-attempt Qwen multiplier; those projections are not semantic validation.
13. **Storage?** Approximately 5.85 GB float32 or 2.92 GB float16 for 1.9M 768-dimensional vectors, plus metadata.
14. **Generation/rebuild time?** Naive measured-throughput projection approximately 21.6 hours for 1.9M senses.
15. **Versioning/storage justified?** Promising, because projected reductions are large, but not finally established until the reference expands.
16. **Deterministic boundary preserved?** Yes. Screening only retains candidates; Python still owns eligible partitions and topology.
17. **Screened-Qwen credible for planning?** Promising enough for a narrowly expanded validation milestone, not production planning yet.
18. **If screening fails later?** Reconsider exhaustive Qwen for small partitions and a simpler bounded/manual escalation or constrained whole-partition alternative for large partitions.

## 14. Complexity decision and next milestone

**Disposition: screening promising but reference too small.**

The measured result clears the initial safety and reduction target, and projected savings are large enough that the embedding artifact may justify its complexity. However, the positive reference count is only four, the classes overlap, and the high-similarity keep-separate cleave cases demonstrate a real semantic boundary risk.

Recommend one narrowly expanded reference-validation milestone before M004.6.6:

- add Product Owner / Architect-approved merge and keep-separate pairs across more words, especially technical, grammatical, inflected, and high-polysemy partitions;
- validate whether threshold 0.90 retains all approved merges without becoming brittle;
- reassess the same simple definition-focused representation before adding model variants.

No production embedding integration is authorized by this closeout.
