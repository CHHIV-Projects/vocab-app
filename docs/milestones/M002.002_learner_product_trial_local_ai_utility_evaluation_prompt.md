## 1. Objective

Evaluate whether the current local-AI synthesis path provides enough trustworthy learner value to justify its role in the product.

This milestone is primarily an **evaluation milestone**, not a semantic-remediation milestone.

The central questions are:

1. How often does the current GPT-OSS synthesis path produce learner wording that is semantically faithful to the exact source sense(s) it references?
2. How often do failures resemble the M002.001 `lantern` failure: structurally valid output whose wording is attached to the correct canonical IDs but does not accurately represent those sources?
3. How useful are the resulting learner definitions, grouping, Core/Additional prominence, labels, and presentation when they are semantically aligned?
4. What are the practical latency and reliability characteristics of the current pipeline?
5. Is the current local model suitable for:
   - the existing multi-sense scaffold/grouping role;
   - a narrower one-source-sense → one-learner-definition role;
   - wording/ranking assistance only;
   - or no learner-facing semantic role?
6. Would a bounded comparison against newer local models that fit the 16 GB GPU materially change the conclusion?

M002.002 must produce evidence sufficient for M002.003 to make a semantic-policy and production-hardening decision.

## 6. Evaluation Principle

The model must not grade itself.

Semantic-quality conclusions must be based on:
- exact source evidence;
- exact model-stage outputs;
- deterministic structural facts;
- explicit human review where semantic judgment is required.

Automated heuristics may flag suspicious cases, but they must not silently become semantic truth.

Separate at least these layers:

1. **Source fidelity** — does the generated wording accurately represent its exact linked source sense(s)?
2. **Identity fidelity** — are the intended source IDs/POS/coverage structurally preserved?
3. **Grouping quality** — are source senses combined only when the resulting learner meaning remains accurate?
4. **Prominence/ranking quality** — are Core vs Additional choices sensible for a learner?
5. **Presentation usefulness** — is aligned output actually clearer/more useful than source wording?
6. **Operational reliability** — validation failures, retries, latency and variability.

A structurally valid result may still fail source fidelity.

## 7. Phase A — Trial Corpus Design

Before large-scale model runs, create a proposed evaluation corpus and submit it for Product Owner review.

Target final corpus: **50–100 words**, with **60 words preferred** unless a justified early-stop rule is triggered.

The corpus must not be composed solely of known failures or solely of easy words.

Create three explicit strata:

### A. Normal learner-use stratum
Approximately 25–30 words representing ordinary dictionary/vocabulary use:
- common noun/verb/adjective vocabulary;
- modest sense counts;
- mixture of concrete and abstract meanings;
- no deliberate cherry-picking for model success.

### B. Polysemy/complexity stratum
Approximately 20 words with:
- multiple source senses;
- multiple POS where available;
- closely related senses;
- senses that test grouping and alias discipline;
- enough evidence to stress multi-item alignment.

### C. Known challenge/regression stratum
Approximately 10 words, including:
- `lantern`;
- representative prior observed words such as `jelly`, `cobble`, `phone`, `vertigo`, `flummox`, `quartz`, `archipelago` where lexical evidence is available and relevant;
- additional words only when their reason for inclusion is recorded.

For every proposed word record:
- normalized lemma;
- source sense count by POS;
- total source sense count;
- corpus stratum;
- inclusion rationale.

Prefer deterministic selection rules and current lexical metadata over arbitrary Coder preference.

Submit the proposed corpus through Repo Control for PO review before executing the full run.

## 9. Phase C — Current GPT-OSS Baseline

Evaluate the current production synthesis contract/model first.

For each word capture:
- lookup/evidence success;
- source senses/POS;
- structural synthesis success/failure;
- scaffold alias coverage;
- scaffold semantic alignment;
- grouping result;
- final learner meanings;
- linked source IDs;
- Core/Additional placement;
- total and stage latency;
- whether cache was hit or fresh generation occurred.

### Semantic classification

For each generated learner meaning classify against its exact linked source sense(s):

- **ALIGNED** — accurate learner paraphrase of the linked source.
- **QUALIFIED** — broadly related but overbroad, underspecified, unnecessarily embellished, or not clearly entailed.
- **MISALIGNED** — wording describes a different source sense or materially changes meaning.
- **UNSUPPORTED** — introduces substantive content not supported by linked evidence.
- **UNJUDGED** — human semantic review not yet completed.

Also separately flag:
- omitted source meaning;
- duplicate semantic meaning;
- cross-sense shift;
- cross-POS semantic contamination;
- incorrect grouping;
- questionable Core/Additional prominence;
- model validation rejection.

Do not collapse these into one generic "failure" bucket.
